## 4. Building an Inspection Solution From Scratch

My general development process followed these stages:

### Step 1 — Understand the Inspection Requirement

Identify the visual feature, defect, or condition that the inspection must evaluate.

Determine what should be considered an acceptable result and what should trigger a failure.

### Step 2 — Configure Image Acquisition

Establish an appropriate image source and verify that the acquired images are suitable for the intended inspection.

Consider image clarity, positioning, and consistency before developing the inspection logic.

### Step 3 — Select and Configure Inspection Tools

Choose suitable VisionMaster tools based on the inspection requirement.

Depending on the task, this could involve traditional image-processing operations, recognition tools, or AI-based methods.

### Step 4 — Define Regions of Interest

Create or adjust ROIs to focus inspection processing on relevant areas of the image.

Refine the selected regions when necessary to improve consistency and reduce irrelevant detections.

### Step 5 — Build the Inspection Workflow

Connect the required tools into a functional inspection sequence.

Configure the flow of image data, processing outputs, inspection decisions, and final results.

### Step 6 — Test and Evaluate Detection

Run the solution against available inspection examples.

Observe how the workflow responds to acceptable conditions, defects, and variations in image appearance.

### Step 7 — Troubleshoot and Optimize

Investigate incorrect results, including false calls and escapes.

Review image conditions, ROI placement, tool behavior, parameter settings, and workflow logic before making adjustments.

### Step 8 — Validate and Integrate Results

Retest the updated solution and verify that inspection outcomes can be communicated to external software.

Participate in on-site testing where required.

This process was iterative rather than strictly linear. Inspection development frequently required returning to earlier stages to improve behavior.

## 5. Rule-Based and AI-Based Inspection

One important part of my experience was learning to work with different inspection approaches.

**Rule-based inspection** uses explicitly configured image-processing operations and decision criteria. It can be useful for structured visual checks where relevant features and expected conditions can be defined.

**AI-based inspection** uses trained models or AI inspection tools to evaluate visual patterns that may be difficult to describe through fixed rules alone.

I worked with both approaches in VisionMaster and selected or combined tools according to the inspection requirements.

This experience helped me understand that the effectiveness of an inspection solution depends not only on the chosen algorithm, but also on image acquisition, ROI configuration, workflow design, parameter selection, and validation.

## 6. Inspection Testing and Troubleshooting

A significant part of my work involved evaluating whether inspection solutions behaved correctly under different test conditions.

Two important concepts were:

- **False call:** An acceptable product or feature is incorrectly identified as defective.
- **Escape:** A defective product or feature is incorrectly accepted.

When investigating inspection inconsistencies, I reviewed the relevant image conditions, inspection regions, tool outputs, parameter settings, and workflow behavior.

I then adjusted the configuration where appropriate and repeated testing to evaluate the effect of the changes.

This process developed my skills in structured troubleshooting, observation, root-cause investigation, and iterative improvement.

No confidential production statistics or customer-specific acceptance thresholds are disclosed in this document.
